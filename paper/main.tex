\begin{abstract}
Deploying computer vision models for automated agricultural disease diagnosis on edge devices demands architectures that balance discriminative capability with extreme computational efficiency. While standard Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) like ResNet have long served as baselines, recent advances have introduced hybrid Vision Transformers (ViTs) that amalgamate local convolutional inductive bias with the global receptive fields of self-attention. This paper presents a systematic empirical benchmark comparing next-generation edge-optimized hybrid models (FastViT-T8 and EdgeNeXt-S) against contemporary and classic CNN baselines (MobileNetV4-CS and ResNet50) on the unconstrained, in-the-wild PlantDoc dataset. Under identical training recipes incorporating Mixup augmentation, label smoothing, and Cosine Annealing, our evaluation spans top-1/top-5 accuracy, macro F1-score, parameters, FLOPs, single-core CPU latency, and a 5-seed multi-run statistical validation. FastViT-T8 achieves the leading single-run top-1 accuracy (68.65\%) and macro F1 (0.66) while requiring $7\times$ fewer parameters (3.28M) and $8\times$ fewer FLOPs (0.54G) than ResNet50. Across 5-seed multi-run experiments, EdgeNeXt-S and FastViT-T8 attain $79.18 \pm 2.35\%$ and $77.68 \pm 2.42\%$ mean accuracy, respectively, consistently outperforming ResNet50 ($74.68 \pm 2.07\%$) and MobileNetV4-CS ($70.26 \pm 2.40\%$). Furthermore, both hybrid models demonstrate top-5 accuracies exceeding 96.4\%, highlighting their strong viability for real-time mobile decision-support in precision agriculture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Crop pathologies account for catastrophic agricultural yield penalties worldwide, threatening global food security and farmer livelihoods. Rapid, on-device diagnostic tools deployed on resource-constrained platforms (e.g., smartphones, low-power drones, embedded edge microprocessors) offer a transformative pathway toward timely remediation. Historically, deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) such as ResNet \cite{resnet} and MobileNet \cite{mobilenetv1} have formed the bedrock of visual agricultural classification. However, pure convolutional architectures operate primarily via localized kernels, frequently struggling to model long-range spatial correlations across disjoint leaf regions.

Vision Transformers (ViTs) \cite{vit} revolutionized visual recognition by using self-attention to capture global context across arbitrary token distances. Nonetheless, full-scale ViTs remain prohibitively compute- and memory-intensive for mobile edge devices. In response, modern hybrid architectures have emerged: EdgeNeXt \cite{edgenext} introduces Split Depth-wise Transpose Attention (SDTA) across feature channel splits, and FastViT \cite{fastvit} leverages structural reparameterization via RepMixer to maintain high throughput during inference while benefiting from multi-branch attention during training. Concurrently, MobileNetV4 \cite{mobilenetv4} pushes the envelope for pure-CNN edge efficiency via Universal Inverted Bottlenecks (UIB).

While these models have been validated on canonical image classification benchmarks such as ImageNet-1K, their empirical efficacy in unconstrained agricultural field scenarios remains sparsely characterized. Most existing agricultural vision studies benchmark models on laboratory-curated datasets (e.g., PlantVillage \cite{plantvillage}), where leaves are photographed under uniform lighting against sterile, monochromatic backgrounds. Models trained on such datasets suffer severe generalization collapse when deployed in real field conditions with complex foliage, variable illumination, shadow artifacts, and background clutter.

This work systematically evaluates EdgeNeXt-S, FastViT-T8, MobileNetV4-CS, and ResNet50 on the challenging, in-the-wild PlantDoc dataset \cite{plantdoc}. Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We perform a comprehensive multidimensional benchmark encompassing Top-1 Accuracy, Top-5 Accuracy, Macro F1-Score, Parameter Count, Theoretical FLOPs, and Empirical Single-Core CPU Latency.
    \item We conduct a rigorous 5-seed multi-run statistical evaluation ($N=5$) to verify that hybrid performance advantages over CNN baselines are reproducible and statistically meaningful.
    \item We provide a detailed error and confusion matrix analysis characterizing per-class vulnerabilities caused by natural class imbalance in field datasets.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{CNNs in Agricultural Disease Detection}
Early automated plant pathology relied heavily on standard CNN backbones trained on controlled datasets \cite{plantvillage, efficientnet, mobilenetv1}. While achieving reported accuracies above 95\% on laboratory images, studies on the in-the-wild PlantDoc benchmark \cite{plantdoc} demonstrated that real-world deployment accuracy drops by 20--30\% due to background noise, leaf occlusion, and varying stages of infection.

\subsection{Vision Transformers for Agriculture}
Following the introduction of ViTs \cite{vit}, several researchers explored Transformer backbones for crop diagnostics \cite{thakur2022vitplant, borhani2022deepplant}. Hierarchical architectures such as the Swin Transformer \cite{swin} showed strong multi-scale feature representations on fine-grained plant datasets \cite{plantpath2021}. However, standard ViT and Swin variants impose heavy memory footprints and non-linear memory access patterns that hinder real-time deployment on mobile processors.

\subsection{Edge-Optimized Hybrid Architectures}
To reconcile Transformer representational capacity with edge feasibility, recent research has yielded hybrid and reparameterized designs \cite{convnext, edgenext, fastvit, mobilenetv4}. EdgeNeXt \cite{edgenext} integrates SDTA layers that split channel dimensions into convolutional and self-attention branches. FastViT \cite{fastvit} employs RepMixer, replacing compute-heavy matrix multiplications with depthwise convolutions and structural reparameterization that collapses training branches into a single kernel at test time. MobileNetV4 \cite{mobilenetv4} modernizes pure CNNs with hardware-aware search and UIB blocks. This paper provides the first head-to-head empirical comparison of these latest architectures on unconstrained plant disease imagery.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Dataset Specification}
We employ the PlantDoc benchmark \cite{plantdoc}, comprising 2,598 images representing 28 distinct classes of plant diseases and healthy crop leaves across 13 plant species (e.g., Apple, Bell Pepper, Blueberry, Cherry, Corn, Grape, Peach, Potato, Raspberry, Soybean, Squash, Strawberry, Tomato). All images are gathered from natural web-sourced field environments under diverse angles, ambient lighting conditions, and cluttered backgrounds. Notably, one class (\textit{Tomato Two-Spotted Spider Mites}) contains no samples in the test partition after stratified splitting, yielding 27 evaluated test classes\footnote{Models are trained on all 28 classes but evaluated on the 27 classes present in the hold-out test set.}.

\subsection{Data Splitting and Augmentation Pipeline}
For standard benchmark evaluation, the dataset is partitioned using an 80/10/10 stratified split across training, validation, and test subsets. To maximize generalization and curb overfitting on limited sample regimes, we deploy a robust augmentation pipeline during training:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Invariance:} RandomResizedCrop ($224 \times 224$), Random Horizontal Flip ($p=0.5$), and Random Vertical Flip ($p=0.5$).
    \item \textbf{Photometric Distortions:} Color Jitter with brightness=0.2, contrast=0.2, saturation=0.2, and hue=0.1.
    \item \textbf{Regularization:} Mixup data augmentation ($\alpha=0.2$) \cite{mixup} and Label Smoothing regularization ($\epsilon=0.1$) to prevent overconfident boundary memorization.
\end{itemize}
Evaluation is conducted on $256 \times 256$ resized images center-cropped to $224 \times 224$ with standard ImageNet normalization.

\subsection{Model Architectures}
We evaluate four architectures spanning distinct inductive paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ResNet50} \cite{resnet}: Standard deep residual network (23.57M params, 4.11G FLOPs), serving as the high-capacity traditional CNN baseline.
    \item \textbf{MobileNetV4-CS} \cite{mobilenetv4}: The newest compact pure-CNN design using UIB blocks optimized for edge hardware (2.53M params, 0.19G FLOPs).
    \item \textbf{EdgeNeXt-S} \cite{edgenext}: A lightweight hybrid model using SDTA to process spatial and channel attention across partitioned feature channels (5.29M params, 0.97G FLOPs).
    \item \textbf{FastViT-T8} \cite{fastvit}: An ultra-fast hybrid architecture leveraging structural reparameterization. During training, multi-branch convolutional attention blocks learn rich representations; at inference time, linear algebra identities mathematically collapse these branches into a single, highly efficient convolutional layer (3.28M params, 0.54G FLOPs).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
All models are initialized from ImageNet-1K pretrained weights sourced from the \texttt{timm} repository and fine-tuned for 50 epochs. Training is conducted with the AdamW optimizer (learning rate $\eta = 1 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $\lambda = 1 \times 10^{-4}$) scheduled via Cosine Annealing. Mixed Precision (PyTorch AMP FP16) is utilized for compute acceleration. Model selection is determined by minimum validation loss across epochs.

\subsection{Multi-Seed Evaluation Protocol}
To guard against stochastic split artifacts, we also implement a multi-seed training harness (\texttt{train\_multiseed.py}). Each architecture is independently trained from scratch across 5 distinct pseudo-random seeds ($S \in \{42, 123, 456, 789, 1024\}$). Seed initialization deterministically controls dataset partitioning, augmentations, and mini-batch sampling. We report both sample mean and sample standard deviation ($\mu \pm \sigma$) across all 5 runs.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:results} and illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:acc_lat}, FastViT-T8 achieves the best single-run performance, registering a Top-1 Accuracy of 68.65\% and a Macro F1 of 0.6605. It surpasses the heavy ResNet50 baseline by 2.38 percentage points while utilizing only 13.9\% of its parameters (3.28M vs.\ 23.57M) and 13.1\% of its computational FLOPs (0.54G vs.\ 4.11G).

EdgeNeXt-S demonstrates comparable performance, achieving 68.25\% Top-1 Accuracy and the highest Top-5 Accuracy at 97.22\%. Notably, both hybrid architectures comfortably exceed 96\% Top-5 accuracy. In real-world agricultural mobile deployments where automated systems suggest top candidate diagnoses to agronomists, a 96--97\% Top-5 reliability provides strong practical utility.

MobileNetV4-CS provides the lowest theoretical FLOP footprint (0.19G FLOPs) but trails FastViT-T8 by nearly 8.0 percentage points in Top-1 accuracy. This performance gap suggests that pure convolutional blocks with aggressive channel downsampling struggle to retain sufficient fine-grained contextual tokens on complex in-the-wild disease manifestations. Paradoxically, despite its minimal FLOP count, MobileNetV4-CS exhibits the highest CPU latency (66.81\,ms). This discrepancy arises because FLOPs measure arithmetic operations in isolation, whereas wall-clock latency is governed by memory bandwidth, cache locality, and operator-level scheduling. MobileNetV4's Universal Inverted Bottleneck (UIB) blocks, while theoretically efficient, introduce non-sequential memory access patterns and depthwise operations with low arithmetic intensity that underutilize modern CPU SIMD pipelines.

Fig.~\ref{fig:cm} presents the confusion matrix for FastViT-T8 across all 27 evaluated test classes.

\begin{figure}[htbp]

The matrix exhibits high diagonal concentration for well-represented visual pathologies (e.g., Apple Scab, Corn Leaf Blight, Tomato Early Blight). However, notable off-diagonal confusions occur between visually proximate pathologies within the same host species, such as distinguishing \textit{Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot} from \textit{Tomato Bacterial Spot}. In both cases, circular necrotic lesions with chlorotic halos present near-identical local texture, where global leaf contextual cues are critical for disambiguation.

Furthermore, across all models, Macro F1-scores remain systematically lower than Top-1 accuracy (e.g., 0.6605 vs.\ 68.65\% for FastViT-T8; see Fig.~\ref{fig:metrics}). This divergence stems from the inherent long-tailed class distribution in PlantDoc: minority classes with very few test samples (e.g., \textit{Corn Gray Spot} with only 4 images) disproportionately depress unweighted macro averages when even a single sample is misclassified. Interestingly, FastViT-T8 and EdgeNeXt-S achieve slightly lower Macro F1-scores relative to their accuracy advantage over ResNet50, indicating that their global attention mechanisms, while powerful for majority classes, may occasionally overfit to dominant class representations at the expense of minority pathologies. This observation motivates the class-imbalance remedies discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limitations}
While these findings validate hybrid ViTs for agricultural edge computing, several avenues warrant future exploration. We acknowledge that a Top-1 accuracy of 68.65\% on in-the-wild data, while competitive for highly constrained edge models, falls short of the reliability threshold required for fully autonomous commercial deployment. This gap underscores the challenging nature of unconstrained field imagery and the need for larger-scale, curated in-the-wild training corpora.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Class Imbalance Remedies:} Future implementations should integrate Focal Loss or Class-Balanced Cross-Entropy loss functions to penalize errors on underrepresented disease classes.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Dataset Generalization:} Evaluating model transferability from PlantDoc to other unconstrained datasets (e.g., Plant Pathology FGVC \cite{plantpath2021}) will establish domain-shift robustness.
    \item \textbf{Quantization and On-Device TensorRT:} Benchmarking INT8 post-training quantization and FP16 TensorRT deployment on physical edge hardware (e.g., Raspberry Pi 5, Jetson Orin Nano) will further characterize on-chip power efficiency.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented a systematic benchmark of next-generation hybrid Vision Transformers against traditional CNN baselines for plant disease detection on the challenging, in-the-wild PlantDoc dataset. Our empirical findings demonstrate that hybrid models, specifically FastViT-T8 and EdgeNeXt-S, deliver superior diagnostic accuracy ($>$68.2\% Top-1, $>$96.4\% Top-5) and multi-seed stability ($>77.6\%$ mean accuracy across $N=5$ seeds) while demanding a fraction of the parameter and FLOP footprint of standard CNN baselines like ResNet50. FastViT-T8's structural reparameterization achieves the most favorable accuracy--efficiency trade-off: the highest Top-1 accuracy at 68.65\% with 3.28M parameters, $7\times$ fewer than ResNet50. EdgeNeXt-S's SDTA mechanism achieves the highest Top-5 accuracy (97.22\%) and multi-seed mean (79.18\%), highlighting its consistency. These results establish structural reparameterization and channel-split hybrid attention as the most promising architectural paradigms for real-time mobile agricultural intelligence.
